Suggested programmes

Three recitals I offer to presenters ready-made. Each can be adjusted, and each link opens the performance enquiry with the programme already chosen.

62m, solo

Two Kinds of Alone

Bach Suite No. 1 · Renko, Fenwold · Bach Suite No. 5

Bach's first suite is the most sociable piece anyone ever wrote for one person: it keeps suggesting a second voice that isn't there. Fenwold puts an actual second presence on stage, a recording of an empty room, and finds out it is lonelier than silence. The fifth suite comes last because it has stopped pretending to have company. The programme runs without an interval and works best in a room small enough to hear the endpin.

58m, with piano

French Rooms

Fauré Élégie · Debussy Sonata · Oyelaran, Six Small Weathers · Fauré Sonata Op. 109

Fauré at both ends, twenty-three years of his life apart, and in the middle the Debussy, which is eleven minutes of a composer refusing to settle into anything. Adaeze Oyelaran's Six Small Weathers was written knowing it would sit beside these pieces, and it borrows their manners without their accent. It needs a good piano and a pianist who likes being argued with.

64m, with piano

The Long Nineteenth

Beethoven Op. 69 · Schumann Fantasiestücke · Brahms Op. 38

Three composers who each knew the one before, in the order they would have wanted. Beethoven's A major sonata is where the cello stops being the piano's accompanist; Schumann's pieces are what that freedom sounded like to a man who had a lot to say quickly; Brahms's E minor sonata is the reply, slower, and looking backwards on purpose. It is the programme I recommend to a series whose audience has been told the cello is a romantic instrument and would like to know why.
