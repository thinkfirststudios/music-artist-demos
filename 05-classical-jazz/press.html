She performs as a soloist and concerto player across North America and Europe, is founding cellist of the Quarry Street Trio, and is Senior Lecturer in Cello at Ashfield Conservatory. 250 words · 239 words — The standard concert programme biography

Odile Rannoch has spent eighteen years building recital programmes that put very old music next to very new music and refuse to apologise for either. A Bach suite against a work written last year; Fauré beside a piece that borrows his manners without his accent. Presenters book her for the argument between them.

Based in Boston, she plays around sixty engagements a year: solo recitals, concerto appearances, and chamber programmes with the Quarry Street Trio, the piano trio she founded in 2016 with violinist Petra Solheim and pianist Hélène Barraud. She works regularly in New York, Montréal and London.

New work is central to what she does. She has commissioned and premiered pieces by Ilya Renko, Adaeze Oyelaran and Marisa Quill, and will give the first performance of Tobias Wrenn's cello concerto Lamp Black in May 2027.

She records for Fenwold, the imprint she founded and runs: Suites in an Empty Hall (2023), three Bach suites recorded over four nights in an empty room, and Late Trains (2026), French and French-adjacent sonatas with Hélène Barraud. She writes her own programme notes.

She is Senior Lecturer in Cello at Ashfield Conservatory, keeps a small private studio for advanced, pre-professional and returning adult players, and gives masterclasses and short residencies. Alongside her own work she records as a session cellist for other artists and for screen. She plays a 1911 cello by the Milanese maker Orsolina Bertelli, on long-term private loan.

450 words · 460 words — For features and grant applications

Odile Rannoch has spent eighteen years building recital programmes that put very old music next to very new music and refuse to apologise for either. A typical evening sets one of Bach's suites against a piece written the previous spring, and the pairing is meant to argue: about what a single melodic line can hold, about silence, about how much of a performance belongs to the room.

She grew up in a household where the radio was always on and the cello was her own idea. She studied in Boston and in London and began her professional life as a chamber player, which she still considers the best training for everything else. Since then she has built a career across solo recitals, concerto appearances and chamber music, playing around sixty engagements a year across North America and Europe, with regular seasons in New York, Montréal and London.

In 2016 she founded the Quarry Street Trio with violinist Petra Solheim and pianist Hélène Barraud. The trio programmes one canonical work and one written in the last decade in every concert, and has now played together long enough, as she puts it, to argue about tempo by email instead of in rehearsal. Barraud is also her regular recital partner.

Commissioning is central to the work rather than an occasional project. Rannoch has commissioned and premiered Ilya Renko's Fenwold for cello and fixed media, Adaeze Oyelaran's Six Small Weathers for cello and piano, and Marisa Quill's Three Letters Not Sent for cello and voice, often through consortia of presenters who share the fee and each give a regional premiere. In May 2027 she gives the first performance of Tobias Wrenn's cello concerto Lamp Black.

She records for Fenwold, the imprint she founded and runs because, as she says, nobody else was going to make these records. Suites in an Empty Hall (2023) presents three of the Bach suites recorded over four nights in an empty hall, with no audience and no edits longer than a phrase. Late Trains (2026), with Hélène Barraud, pairs Debussy, Fauré and Janáček with Oyelaran's Six Small Weathers.

Unusually, she writes her own programme notes, and presenters are welcome to reprint them. The notes, which range from the practicalities of retuning a cello for Bach's fifth suite to the experience of playing against a recording of an empty room, have become a small body of writing in their own right.

She is Senior Lecturer in Cello at Ashfield Conservatory in Boston, keeps a small private studio for advanced, pre-professional and returning adult players, and gives masterclasses and short residencies. She also records as a session cellist for other artists' records and for screen.

She plays a cello made in Milan in 1911 by Orsolina Bertelli, on long-term private loan.

3 · Requirements

Technical and practical requirements
StageRaked or flat. A stable armless chair of playing height, and a low platform if the hall is flat.
FloorA cello endpin stop or a rug. I travel with both, but ask anyway.
PianoFor any piano programme: the house piano make, model and last tuning date, and a tuning on the day.
Instrument roomA warm, lockable room for the instrument, and no HVAC vent aimed at the stage.
On stageA music stand and a stand light.
TicketsTwo complimentary tickets.
TravelFrom Boston.

If any of this is a problem, say so — it usually isn't, and every one of these has been a problem once.
